What a Software Passport means

A Software Passport is a dated evidence record for a software asset. It can describe identity, security signals, dependencies, provenance, reliability indicators, observations and trust state.

Important: A Passport, score, report, badge or finding is not a security certification, legal opinion, compliance certification, warranty, or guarantee of future software behavior.

Methodology

  1. Identify the software asset and available identity signals.
  2. Collect observable evidence such as SBOMs, vulnerability results, repository signals, attestations and supplied documents.
  3. Verify selected evidence where independent verification is available.
  4. Assess evidence and expose reason codes, findings and limitations.
  5. Re-observe over time because software trust is time-dependent.

Missing evidence remains visible as UNKNOWN rather than being converted into a claim of safety.

Security

  • HTTPS/TLS for network transport.
  • Authentication and role-based authorization.
  • Workspace and client scoping.
  • Input validation and rate limiting on sensitive endpoints.
  • Audit logging and evidence-integrity controls.
  • Operational monitoring and error reporting where configured.

SPR does not claim SOC 2, ISO 27001, penetration-test certification, government approval, or regulatory certification unless a specific current claim is separately documented.

Third-party dependencies

Parts of the Service rely on providers for hosting, authentication, billing, source-code integrations, vulnerability data, optional AI assistance and monitoring. These dependencies are disclosed in the Privacy Policy and are subject to their own availability and terms.

Digital Product Passport distinction

SPR is not the European Commission's Digital Product Passport registry. “Passport” is the product's term for its software evidence record. SPR should not be represented as an EU government registry or certification authority.
